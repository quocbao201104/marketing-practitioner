# Use Cases

Marketing Practitioner can help carry a marketing assignment from evidence and decisions through to usable content, revisions, and learning. Bring the part you need done now: a focused copy edit can stay focused, while a larger brief can connect several kinds of work.

These use cases apply existing Marketing Practitioner guidance; they do not introduce separate modules. Coverage describes available knowledge and how it can be combined, not demonstrated runtime performance.

The briefs are illustrative, informed by public job descriptions and the project's recent research. They are not completed client projects or measured success claims. The [inventory and evidence](docs/use-case-inventory.md) maps the assignments to existing capabilities, sources, and boundaries, distinguishing directly requested work from derived examples.

## Research and strategic choices

You can ask to understand the evidence without also requesting a strategy or campaign. Supply your learning question and the relevant sources or research access.

| Assignment | Useful result | Example request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare customer interviews | A bounded learning question, participant criteria, topics, and follow-up prompts | “We need to understand why customers abandon setup. Draft an interview guide around their actual experience, using these known product constraints.” |
| Synthesize customer evidence | Source-linked patterns, conflicting accounts, alternatives, barriers, and unanswered questions | “Analyze these interviews and support notes. Show what the evidence supports, where accounts differ, and what we still need to learn.” |
| Produce a competitor snapshot | A sourced comparison of audience, positioning, offer, proof, and possible differentiation | “Review these three competitors. Compare how they describe their value and offer, and identify plausible gaps with source links.” |
| Choose an audience and positioning | Comparable options and a recommendation with decisive trade-offs | “Using this customer evidence, compare our two candidate segments and recommend an initial position. Explain what would change your recommendation.” |

Interview preparation does not itself recruit participants or conduct conversations. Public competitor claims describe what competitors say; they do not verify customer experience or market share. For a complete evidence-to-launch assignment, see [case 7](#7-turn-market-and-customer-evidence-into-a-launch-narrative).

## Writing, editing, and creative development

A sufficient brief can go straight to writing. Include the reader, purpose, facts, selected message, and any wording or terms that must stay fixed.

| Assignment | Useful result | Example request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Write from an approved brief | The requested caption, email, announcement, headline options, or other bounded copy | “Write one launch email from this approved brief, including subject and preview text. Keep the offer and product claims fixed.” |
| Rewrite in an established voice | Clearer, shorter, or more natural copy that preserves meaning and recognizable voice | “Tighten this founder post using these writing samples. Keep the experience and opinion exactly as supported; do not add anecdotes.” |
| Review copy and its proof | Specific claim, hierarchy, proof, or reader-fit problems with supported corrections | “Critique this landing-page copy against our product facts and evidence. Rewrite the weak sections and identify claims we cannot currently support.” |
| Develop distinct creative concepts | Different ideas or angles, a reasoned selection, and scripts or copy for the chosen direction | “Propose four different ad concepts for this product and audience. Explain their trade-offs, recommend one, and write its script.” |
| Write a customer story | A coherent account of the customer's situation, action, and supported result | “Turn this interview and the approved outcome records into a customer story. Preserve exact quotations and distinguish reported outcomes from demonstrated effects.” |
| Develop a sourced article or buyer comparison | A reader-facing explanation or comparison grounded in relevant sources and supported product differences | “Write a comparison page from these product documents and current competitor sources. Help the specified buyer evaluate fit, attribute external claims, and leave unsupported differences unresolved.” |

Creative alternatives are proposals, not measured winners. Customer stories need real source material and appropriate publication permissions. Filming, image generation, and design-file production depend on the host's tools. Product-state writing has its own useful boundaries in [case 4](#4-write-interface-copy-that-reflects-product-behavior).

## Content, channels, and community

The same source can serve different jobs in a feed, an email, or a discussion. Bring the audience, destination, actual relationship, source material, and relevant channel constraints.

| Assignment | Useful result | Example request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan recurring content | Topics and formats tied to audience needs, a workable sequence/calendar, and useful observations | “Plan a month of content from these customer questions and our available material. Explain each item's job and what response would be useful to observe.” |
| Repurpose a source across channels | Different representations that preserve the source while fitting each audience and interaction | “Turn this webinar transcript into a LinkedIn post, an Instagram carousel outline, and an email. Give each a useful purpose without inventing claims.” |
| Prepare community participation | An introduction, discussion prompt, or reply suited to the community's rules and your actual standing | “Draft an introduction to this Facebook Group using its rules and our relationship to the members. Explain the product concretely and make the feedback request easy to answer.” |
| Brief a creator collaboration | Audience/job, allowed claims, demonstration ideas, creative room, delivery context, and review criteria | “Write a creator brief from these product facts and agreed campaign conditions. Separate what must be accurate from what the creator can interpret.” |

Current destination rules can change the permitted content or ask. Drafting a participation plan does not authorize posting, impersonating a member, or moderating a community. Creator recruitment, contracting, payment, rights clearance, and production remain separate work; paying a creator does not by itself establish paid-ad delivery.

## Brand identity

Bring resolved positioning, existing assets where relevant, and the contexts where the identity must work. These assignments concern persistent identifying cues and their relationships. Visual creation and inspection need appropriate host tools; production formats and rights require verification.

| Assignment | Useful result | Example request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explore and compare identity directions | Distinct visual hypotheses, identifying cues, and a comparison against the brief and required uses | “Develop three identity directions from this approved positioning. Show how they differ and compare them in the deployment contexts we actually need.” |
| Decide whether to preserve, evolve, or replace | A recommendation grounded in existing assets, recognition evidence, and observed problems | “Review our current identity before a redesign. Separate demonstrated problems from preferences and recommend what to preserve or change.” |
| Refine an adopted identity | A targeted correction that keeps the selected direction intact | “Keep this approved mark's identity, but address the identifying opening that disappears at the required small size. Explain and check the proposed change.” |
| Document an identity handoff | Established master/variant relationships, color/type roles, usage constraints, and unresolved applications | “Using these verified masters and agreed rules, prepare a concise identity handoff. Mark unresolved uses instead of inventing minimum sizes or clear-space rules.” |

Exploring a new identity, deciding whether an old one should change, and refining a selected direction are different assignments. Once those decisions are fixed, routine export or resizing is ordinary production work. An attractive preview does not establish a production-ready master, learned recognition, or legal clearance.

## Pages and communication continuity

| Assignment | Useful result | Example request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structure a landing page | Information sequence, proof placement, action/form rationale, mobile reading order, and requested copy | “Use our approved message, evidence, and offer to structure a demo-request page. Explain each section's job and write the copy.” |
| Check a message-to-page handoff | Specific mismatches in promise, qualification, terms, or next action, with targeted repairs | “Review this email subject, body, CTA, and destination page together. Repair any change in the promise or conditions while preserving valid wording.” |

A page's structure can be the open question even when its strategy is settled. Continuity means preserving meaning across touchpoints, not repeating identical words. Whole-site disposition and lifecycle contact decisions are covered in [case 2](#2-audit-a-website-and-decide-what-to-keep) and [case 3](#3-plan-onboarding-renewal-and-reactivation-communication).

## Measurement, experiments, and learning

Specify the decision you need to make and provide the measurement definitions, comparison, timing, and available records. Planning a test, interpreting one, and retaining learning can each be complete assignments.

| Assignment | Useful result | Example request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design a marketing experiment | A decision-linked hypothesis, comparison, eligible population, outcomes, guardrails, and interpretation plan | “Design a test of this new page explanation. Use our traffic constraints and define what would justify adoption, rejection, or an inconclusive result.” |
| Interpret test results | A supported decision or inconclusive finding, with uncertainty and validity limits | “Review this test design and result export. Assess the business relevance, guardrails, and any validity problem before recommending rollout.” |
| Explain paid-media reports | A reconciliation of metric/event definitions, reporting periods, attribution and delivery evidence, plus a next check or bounded recommendation | “These platform and analytics reports disagree. Explain what each measures and what the discrepancy does or does not justify changing.” |
| Turn a campaign into reusable learning | A record connecting prior belief, decision, result, revised belief, scope, and revisit conditions | “Write a postmortem from these briefs, change records, and results. State what we learned, what remains unresolved, and when the finding may stop applying.” |

Experiment implementation, tracking configuration, account access, and spending require external tools and authority. Attribution does not establish incremental impact. Learning persists only when the host retains the relevant records. For a diagnosis that continues into revision and subsequent learning, see [case 1](#1-decide-what-to-change-after-performance-shifts).

## 1. Decide what to change after performance shifts

An ad attracts more clicks but fewer qualified enquiries. An email's tracked opens fall. A page appears to convert worse after a release. These assignments need both interpretation and a decision about what to do next.

Use Marketing Practitioner to connect:

1. **The observation:** what changed, for which audience, version, period, and measurement conditions?
2. **The decision:** does the evidence support a content repair, a delivery or measurement check, a test, waiting, or keeping the current version?
3. **The work:** revise the implicated copy or page section, develop meaningfully different creative candidates, or specify a comparison with useful success measures and guardrails.
4. **The learning:** record what the result supports and where it can be reused.

The useful output is a next-action recommendation with its evidence, plus the requested revision or test plan. A higher click rate alone does not settle whether the creative serves the business objective.

**Example request:** “Review these ad versions, delivery changes, and lead-quality results. Recommend what to do next. If a creative change is justified, draft two distinct alternatives while keeping the audience, offer, and supported claims fixed.”

## 2. Audit a website and decide what to keep

Bring a bounded set of pages, their intended readers and purposes, current product facts, and available usage or search evidence. The work can cover gaps, stale claims, redundancy, inconsistent messaging, and relationships between pages.

The recommendation depends on what each page does:

| Example finding | Decision to investigate |
| --- | --- |
| A low-traffic security page is used during enterprise procurement | Whether it still supplies the proof those buyers need |
| A pricing page and billing-help page use similar vocabulary | Whether they serve different readers and tasks |
| Two guides serve the same task but contain different useful details | Whether one can absorb the necessary coverage and dependencies before consolidation |

Ask for a page-by-page disposition, the reason and evidence for it, priorities, and any dependencies that must be resolved before changing or removing content. The assignment can continue into selected copy repairs and a check that those repairs address the original findings. Redirects, CMS changes, and technical migration work require the appropriate implementation tools and authority.

**Example request:** “Audit these pages before our redesign. Recommend what to keep, repair, combine, or retire, and explain why. Then rewrite the three highest-priority content problems that can be resolved from the supplied material.”

## 3. Plan onboarding, renewal, and reactivation communication

Lifecycle work can start from the customer's current situation: progress, blockers, prior contact, time elapsed, permissions, and applicable terms. Use those facts to decide whether a message is useful and what it should ask the recipient to do.

A sequence can branch when the customer completes a task, changes eligibility, responds, or no longer needs the message. It can also wait, stop, or suppress contact. If renewal, an upgrade offer, and a service notice overlap, the assignment can reconcile their timing and competing asks before drafting copy.

Useful deliverables include a communication plan with entry and exit conditions, message allocation across available channels, draft emails or in-app messages, and the observations needed to assess the program. An unresolved discount or migration policy needs a commercial decision; a churn-risk score alone does not determine a retention treatment.

CRM configuration, trigger implementation, message scheduling, and campaign execution require the appropriate external systems and authority.

**Example request:** “Using these trial milestones, contact history, permissions, and approved plans, design the next onboarding and trial-ending messages. Show when to send, wait, or stop, and write the messages. Account for customers who have already completed setup.”

## 4. Write interface copy that reflects product behavior

Supply the screen context, supported system state, actual action effects, and any known recovery path. Marketing Practitioner can turn those inputs into labels, field guidance, errors, confirmations, empty states, and status messages.

Small state differences can require different wording:

- A successfully loaded view with no matching items differs from a view that failed to load. Only the first establishes that the current result set is empty.
- Cancellation at the end of a paid period differs from immediate loss of access. A confirmation needs to preserve the supplied timing and consequence.
- An error with a supported recovery action differs from one whose cause and recovery are unknown. Helpful wording must not invent a retry or undo capability.

The result can be a revised string set with context and notes on unresolved product facts. Interaction flow, control availability, and product behavior remain inputs to the writing task.

**Example request:** “Rewrite the labels, empty states, and errors in this screen inventory. Use the attached behavior specification. Preserve the action effects, and flag any string that requires a product fact the specification does not establish.”

## 5. Update a campaign after an approved decision changes

Suppose the product's category or eligibility rule changes after the website, launch email, comparison page, and localized assets have been prepared. The work is to trace which meanings depend on the change and repair the affected current representations.

| Material in the supplied asset set | Appropriate treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Current headline or comparison claim that depends on the old decision | Revise and check against the new decision |
| Current copy whose meaning remains valid | Preserve |
| Customer quotation, research note, or historical announcement | Preserve its source or historical meaning; distinguish any new present-day framing |
| Approved localized wording | Repair affected meaning while retaining valid local expression |

Ask for updated assets and a compact change record showing what changed, what was preserved, and any unresolved dependency. The task works over the supplied or accessible asset set; the skill does not automatically discover every campaign asset in your organization.

**Example request:** “Our approved category statement changed. Reconcile the attached launch assets with the new decision. Update all affected current claims within the supplied asset set, preserve quotations and historical records, and give me a change summary.”

## 6. Investigate search visibility before rewriting content

A page can be available, retrieved, selected, and represented differently across discovery surfaces. Low traffic or missing AI citations does not by itself identify which part needs work.

With page content and scoped search evidence, Marketing Practitioner can investigate whether the issue concerns access or availability, retrieval and selection, the representation shown to readers, or the meaning of the reported metric. When the evidence identifies a content problem, the work can continue into a title or description candidate, clearer supported explanation, page restructuring, or a recommendation about related pages. When the problem is technical or still unresolved, the useful result is a precise handoff or next check.

**Example request:** “Review these query/page reports and current pages. Decide whether a content change is justified. Draft any supported changes without altering our approved positioning or inventing claims to match search terms. Identify technical questions separately.”

Current provider behavior needs verification when it changes the answer. Content work cannot promise rankings, surfaced snippets, or AI citations.

## 7. Turn market and customer evidence into a launch narrative

Bring interviews, customer notes, competitor material, product facts, and the launch question. The assignment can connect evidence synthesis, target selection, positioning, and the materials needed to explain the product.

A useful chain is **customer situations and alternatives → target and positioning options → a supported recommendation → message and proof → page, email, or social content**. Start wherever the open work begins; approved positioning does not need to be rebuilt just to write an email.

You can receive a source-linked synthesis, comparable positioning options with trade-offs, and launch materials that carry the selected direction consistently. Interview patterns remain scoped to their sources; they do not become market-size estimates or proof of demand.

**Example request:** “Use these interviews and competitor pages to recommend our initial audience and positioning. Explain the decisive trade-off, then carry your recommendation into a landing-page outline and launch email. Mark claims that still need proof.”

## 8. Compare pricing and packaging, then explain the offer

Use Marketing Practitioner when the open question concerns what customers receive, what they pay for, applicable terms, eligibility, or a transition between plans. Supply customer/value evidence and the relevant product, cost, operational, and commercial constraints.

The result can compare feasible package and charging options, recommend a bounded choice, identify what would change that recommendation, and explain the chosen offer through pricing-page copy or a migration email. Existing-customer terms and new-customer offers can be treated separately where the policy requires it.

Financial feasibility remains dependent on valid finance and operations inputs. Competitor prices or stated willingness to pay alone cannot establish an optimal price.

**Example request:** “Compare these per-seat and usage-based proposals against our supplied customer evidence and constraints. Recommend one, identify unresolved assumptions, and draft the plan comparison and existing-customer announcement using the approved transition policy.”

## 9. Adapt a launch for another language and audience

Bring the source assets, target readers and destination, approved claims and terms, and any local language or brand guidance. The work can adapt a landing page, email, or campaign while preserving the underlying product meaning and commercial promise.

Localization can change how the message is expressed, information order, terminology, and the invitation to act. It also needs to preserve who is speaking, their relationship to the reader, and who is responsible for a commitment or remedy. Those choices need evidence about the actual context; a country label is not enough.

Ask for localized artifacts and a short explanation of meaning-sensitive choices or unresolved inputs. Layout checks and publishing depend on available host tools.

**Example request:** “Adapt this approved launch page and email for our Japanese business audience. Use the supplied terminology and relationship context. Preserve the product claims, eligibility, and responsibility for support, while making the wording natural for each touchpoint.”

## 10. Diagnose and repair marketplace product representation

Bring the product and variant facts, listing content, marketplace and seller context, relevant screenshots or exports, and the question you need answered. The assignment can distinguish problems with product identity, offer state, missing or misleading information, discovery, and paid delivery.

For example, weak Amazon conversion can lead to a comparison of listing evidence, traffic composition, offer state, and campaign changes before deciding whether to rewrite the listing. The deliverable can include prioritized findings, corrected product copy, an image/content brief, and checks that require account access or current platform documentation. Variant-specific facts should remain attached to the right variant.

**Example request:** “Audit this Amazon listing using our product facts, screenshots, and search and advertising reports. Recommend what to fix first, draft any justified listing changes, and distinguish content issues from catalog, offer, or paid-delivery questions.”

The skill supplies marketing reasoning and communication guidance. Marketplace access, catalog updates, fulfillment work, and media changes require separate tools and authorization.

## Start with the work you have

Give your agent the desired result, the relevant evidence or assets, and the choices that are already fixed. You can ask for a recommendation, several alternatives, a finished artifact, or a diagnosis followed by a repair when justified. The [Task Specification Guide](TASK-SPECIFICATION-GUIDE.md) can help turn rough notes into a brief.

These cases describe current knowledge coverage and composition, not guaranteed model performance. The model, source quality, accessible context, language competence, and execution tools affect the result. Public job briefs demonstrate the kinds of assignments requested; repository research supports the ownership mapping. Neither substitutes for live evaluation or observed marketing outcomes. See the [inventory and evidence](docs/use-case-inventory.md) for the distinctions and [README](README.md#quick-start) to get started.
