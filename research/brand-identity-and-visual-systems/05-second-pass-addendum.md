# Brand Identity and Visual Systems — Second-Pass Addendum

Status: **SUPPLEMENTAL THEORY RESEARCH — NOT RUNTIME GUIDANCE**

This pass pressure-tests two areas that were underdeveloped in the first synthesis: **identity redesign / equity preservation** and **cross-cultural visual interpretation**. Both matter because a workflow that works only for a brand-new startup identity would be materially incomplete.

---

## 1. Redesign is not new-identity creation with old files attached

A redesign begins from learned and operational state rather than a blank canvas.

The decision problem is therefore better represented as:

```text
CURRENT IDENTITY
+ EVIDENCE OF LEARNED / OPERATIONAL EQUITY
+ CONCRETE DEFECT OR NEW REQUIREMENT
→ PRESERVE / EVOLVE / REPLACE DECISION
→ ONLY THEN REOPEN THE NECESSARY VISUAL DIMENSIONS
```

not:

```text
CURRENT IDENTITY FEELS OLD
→ GENERATE SOMETHING CLEANER
```

### [BI25] Walsh, Winterich & Mittal (2010)

**Title:** Do logo redesigns help or hurt your brand? The role of brand commitment  
**Venue:** Journal of Product & Brand Management, 19(2), 76–84  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1108/10610421011033421

**Supports:**

- consumer response to logo redesign is not uniform;
- greater redesign change can provoke more negative reactions among consumers with stronger brand commitment;
- redesign magnitude and existing brand relationship can therefore change the decision.

**Does not establish:**

- redesign should never be substantial;
- commitment is the only moderator;
- any specific amount of geometric change is universally safe.

### Synthesis

This strengthens the existing-equity gate in the candidate method:

```text
TEAM BOREDOM
!= BUYER-SIDE REASON TO RESET
```

A redesign recommendation should identify the actual defect or changed requirement and then ask whether the affected visual dimensions can be corrected while preserving valuable lineage.

A useful bounded action set remains:

```text
PRESERVE
The current cue is not implicated by the problem or evidence favors continuity.

EVOLVE
The problem is real but recognizable lineage can be retained.

REPLACE
The affected cue itself is the problem, weak/negative/confusing, or incompatible with the required future state, and replacement risk is justified.
```

These are practitioner decision labels, not proposed runtime primitives.

## 2. Redesign evidence should separate three states

The first pass already distinguished learned distinctive-asset evidence from internal intuition. Redesign adds a second separation:

```text
HISTORICAL USE
The organization has used an asset for a long time.

MEASURED LEARNED EQUITY
Relevant buyers recognize / correctly attribute the asset.

OPERATIONAL DEPENDENCY
The asset is embedded in packaging, product UI, signage, templates,
contracts, installed hardware, distribution, or other costly surfaces.
```

These states can coexist but are not interchangeable.

Examples:

```text
long use + weak buyer linkage
→ emotional/internal attachment may be high while market equity is weak

strong buyer linkage + low operational cost
→ market continuity can still justify preservation

weak buyer linkage + huge operational migration cost
→ replacement may be strategically reasonable but implementation cost remains real
```

The visual-identity owner may synthesize these constraints but must not invent financial, operational, or legal costs.

---

## 3. Candidate redesign test

Before opening broad exploration, ask:

```text
1. WHAT CONCRETE PROBLEM REQUIRES CHANGE?
2. WHICH CURRENT ASSET / RELATIONSHIP CAUSES THAT PROBLEM?
3. WHAT EVIDENCE EXISTS ABOUT LEARNED EQUITY?
4. WHAT OPERATIONAL / LEGAL DEPENDENCIES ARE AUTHORITATIVE INPUTS?
5. WHAT IS THE SMALLEST CHANGE THAT CAN SOLVE THE PROBLEM?
6. WHAT RECOGNIZABLE LINEAGE SHOULD SURVIVE IF POSSIBLE?
7. WHAT WOULD JUSTIFY A FULL RESET INSTEAD?
```

This is deliberately asymmetric: a mature identity should not have to prove its right to exist from zero merely because a redesign project begins.

## 4. Cross-cultural visual interpretation should be conditional, not country-first

Visual meaning can vary across markets, but the research does not support a runtime rule such as:

```text
NEW COUNTRY
→ REOPEN THE WHOLE VISUAL IDENTITY
```

The stronger formulation is:

```text
TARGET-MARKET DIFFERENCE
+ PLAUSIBLE VISUAL MEANING / ASSOCIATION / USE DELTA
+ MATERIAL IDENTITY DECISION DELTA
→ LOCALIZATION DEPENDENCY
```

### [BI26] Henderson, Cote, Leong & Schmitt (2003/2009 cross-national program)

**Title:** Cross-National Logo Evaluation Analysis: An Individual-Level Approach  
**Venue:** Marketing Science  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.1080.0462

**Supports:**

- many logo-design dimensions can be evaluated across countries using common constructs;
- consumer responses are not perfectly invariant across national contexts;
- cross-national segments/clusters can differ in response to design characteristics.

**Does not establish:**

- every country requires a localized logo;
- country membership alone predicts an individual viewer’s response;
- a fixed national design stereotype is safe.

### [BI27] Cross-cultural logo-response research (2019)

**Title:** Same design, same response? Investigating natural designs and cultural differences in logo response  
**Venue:** Journal of Product & Brand Management  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1108/JPBM-10-2017-1632

**Supports:**

- some logo characteristics can have broad cross-cultural appeal;
- cultural dimensions can moderate the strength of logo responses;
- portability and local variation are both empirical questions rather than universal assumptions.

**Does not establish:**

- a deterministic mapping from national culture score to logo geometry;
- that naturalistic design is universally optimal.

## 5. Boundary with Chapter 07 / local adaptation

The candidate owner boundary should remain:

```text
VISUAL IDENTITY OWNER
→ owns the asset, system relationship, and open visual decision

CHAPTER 07 / LOCAL ADAPTATION
→ supplies scoped local evidence only when local realization can
   materially change that open decision
```

Examples:

```text
“Launch the approved global logo in Vietnam.”
→ Vietnam alone does not reopen the mark.

“This hand gesture has a materially different local meaning in the target market.”
→ load scoped localization evidence for that still-open interpretation/risk question.

“The global wordmark uses a Latin-only custom construction and the Arabic-market identity needs a native-script relationship.”
→ visual identity remains the system owner;
  language/script realization requires a local/language dependency.
```

The future capability must not create country visual-style packs or infer culture from nationality alone.

---

## 6. Revised identity-change invariant

The second pass strengthens the following candidate invariant:

```text
NEW IDENTITY
→ EXPLORE WHEN THE VISUAL HYPOTHESIS IS OPEN

EXISTING IDENTITY
→ PRESERVE BY DEFAULT THE STATE NOT IMPLICATED BY A REAL PROBLEM
→ REOPEN ONLY THE DIMENSIONS NECESSARY TO SOLVE THAT PROBLEM
```

This matches the repository’s broader resolved-state rule and reduces rebrand drift.
