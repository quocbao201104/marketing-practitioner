# Brand Identity and Visual Systems — Source Map

Status: **WORKING EVIDENCE MAP — NOT A RUNTIME REFERENCE FILE**

This file records the main conceptual and empirical parents used in the first research pass. It is intentionally a source map rather than a claim dump. Each source is listed with the bounded proposition it can support and the inference it must **not** be stretched to support.

---

## Source classes

```text
A. peer-reviewed marketing / consumer research
B. distinctive-asset / brand-memory research
C. official trademark / legal infrastructure
D. professional identity-system practice
E. production / implementation guidance
F. generative-image research
```

No one class is sufficient by itself. A studio case study can demonstrate how a system was built, but not causal marketing superiority. A marketing experiment can identify a perceptual effect under studied conditions, but not provide production geometry or legal clearance.

## A. Logo / visual-design effects in marketing research

### [BI01] Henderson, P. W. & Cote, J. A. (1998)

**Title:** Guidelines for Selecting or Modifying Logos  
**Venue:** Journal of Marketing, 62(2), 14–30  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/002224299806200202

**Supports:**

- logo properties can affect recognition and image objectives;
- naturalness, harmony, and elaborateness do not collapse to a single “simple is good” rule;
- different objectives can imply different desirable visual characteristics.

**Does not establish:**

- a universal scoring formula for all modern brands;
- that the studied design characteristics should become deterministic agent rules;
- current digital-production constraints.

---

### [BI02] Hagtvedt, H. (2011)

**Title:** The Impact of Incomplete Typeface Logos on Perceptions of the Firm  
**Venue:** Journal of Marketing, 75(4), 86–93  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkg.75.4.86

**Supports:**

- deliberate visual incompleteness/ambiguity can trade perceived trustworthiness against innovativeness;
- the same visual treatment can have different effects depending on consumer regulatory focus.

**Does not establish:**

- incomplete typography is inherently innovative or bad;
- a general instruction to use ambiguity for technology brands.

---

### [BI03] Cian, L., Krishna, A. & Elder, R. S. (2014)

**Title:** This Logo Moves Me: Dynamic Imagery from Static Images  
**Venue:** Journal of Marketing Research, 51(2), 184–197  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.13.0023

**Supports:**

- static logo geometry can evoke perceived movement;
- movement can affect engagement and attitudes;
- congruence with brand characteristics moderates the effect.

**Does not establish:**

- motion cues are universally better;
- every “dynamic” brand needs a tilted or directional mark.

---

### [BI04] Jiang, Y., Gorn, G. J., Chattopadhyay, A. & colleagues (2015/2016)

**Title:** Does Your Company Have the Right Logo? How and Why Circular- and Angular-Logo Shapes Influence Brand Attribute Judgments  
**Venue:** Journal of Consumer Research, 42(5), 709–726  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucv049

**Supports:**

- circular versus angular forms can activate different attribute associations;
- effects depend on imagery processing and message context.

**Does not establish:**

- `round = friendly` and `angular = premium` as universal design mappings;
- a deterministic geometry generator from brand adjectives.

---

### [BI05] Luffarelli, J., Stamatogiannakis, A. & Yang, H. (2019)

**Title:** The Visual Asymmetry Effect: An Interplay of Logo Design and Brand Personality on Brand Equity  
**Venue:** Journal of Marketing Research, 56(1), 89–103  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/0022243718820548

**Supports:**

- asymmetry can increase arousal/excitement perceptions;
- brand-personality congruence changes the value of asymmetry.

**Does not establish:**

- asymmetry is intrinsically more distinctive or attractive;
- exciting brands should always use asymmetric marks.

---

### [BI06] Luffarelli, J., Mukesh, M. & Mahmood, A. (2019)

**Title:** Let the Logo Do the Talking: The Influence of Logo Descriptiveness on Brand Equity  
**Venue:** Journal of Marketing Research, 56(5), 862–878  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/0022243719845000

**Supports:**

- descriptive logo content can increase processing fluency, authenticity perceptions, evaluations, intentions, and brand performance in studied settings;
- familiarity and negative category associations materially moderate the effect.

**Does not establish:**

- descriptive marks are always preferable;
- descriptiveness equals legal distinctiveness;
- a literal category icon is automatically ownable.

---

### [BI07] Tang, Q., Huang, X. & Zhang, K. (2025/2026)

**Title:** Simple or complex logos? The impact of logo complexity on brand luxuriousness perception  
**Venue:** International Journal of Research in Marketing, 43(2A), 264–277  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijresmar.2025.04.007

**Supports:**

- greater logo complexity can increase perceived luxuriousness through perceived craftsmanship in studied contexts;
- familiarity moderates the effect;
- simplification is not a universal improvement.

**Does not establish:**

- complex logos are generally superior;
- complexity should be optimized without regard to recognition or deployment.

---

### [BI08] Chen, J., Liu, X., Feng, W. & Meng, L. M. (2025)

**Title:** The visual language of brand logos: Exploring the relationship between logo simplicity and perceptions of brand warmth and competence  
**Venue:** Journal of Business Research  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2025.115580

**Supports:**

- simple versus complex logos can shift warmth/competence perceptions through psychological distance;
- consumption goals and brand personality moderate preferences.

**Does not establish:**

- simple logos are objectively more competent;
- warmth and competence should become a fixed brand-identity ontology.

---

### [BI09] Logo concreteness / perceived brand age research (2025/2026)

**Title:** Visualizing brand longevity: How logo concreteness shapes consumer perceptions of brand age  
**Venue:** Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services, 88 (2026), 104495  
**Publisher URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0969698925002747

**Supports:**

- concreteness and complexity are separable visual constructs;
- concrete versus abstract logos can alter perceived brand age/tradition, with positioning-contingent preference.

**Does not establish:**

- concrete = old-fashioned or abstract = modern as a universal rule.

## B. Distinctive assets / brand memory

### [BI10] Romaniuk / Ehrenberg-Bass — Four Commandments for brand identity

**Source:** Ehrenberg-Bass Institute for Marketing Science  
**URL:** https://marketingscience.info/the-four-commandments-future-proofing-a-brands-identity/

**Supports:**

- distinctive assets are non-name brand cues built through repeated marketing activity;
- brands should avoid assets with strong pre-existing mental competition;
- preservation and continued investment matter once an asset has learned brand linkage.

**Does not establish:**

- a newly generated visual has Fame or Uniqueness;
- distinctiveness is equivalent to visual novelty.

---

### [BI11] Ward et al. / distinctive-asset benchmarking (2026)

**Title:** Shape-based assets are strongest: benchmarking distinctive brand asset performance across industries  
**Venue:** International Journal of Advertising  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/02650487.2026.2637295

**Supports:**

- Fame and Uniqueness remain useful empirical dimensions for asset strength;
- in a large cross-category dataset, shape-based assets such as logos/packaging performed strongly relative to some other asset classes;
- asset-type performance varies and should be treated empirically rather than by folklore.

**Does not establish:**

- shape is always the strongest possible asset for every brand or category;
- color should be ignored.

---

### [BI12] Brus, Hartnett, Faulkner, Driesener et al. (2025/2026)

**Title:** Assessing branding strength: comparing marketer judgement and consumer data for brand identity elements  
**Venue:** Journal of Brand Management, 33, 1–17  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1057/s41262-025-00395-y

**Supports:**

- marketer intuition is a poor substitute for consumer evidence of brand-element Fame and Uniqueness;
- marketers commonly overestimate Fame and misjudge consumer associations;
- identity changes can destroy recognition if existing learned assets are discarded without evidence.

**Does not establish:**

- qualitative expert judgment has zero value;
- a new brand must delay all identity decisions until large-sample survey data exist.

## C. Trademark / official legal infrastructure

### [BI13] WIPO Global Brand Database

**Source:** World Intellectual Property Organization  
**URL:** https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database

**Supports:**

- trademark searching can combine names, goods/services, and image similarity across participating collections;
- image similarity is a legitimate pre-flight discovery tool.

**Does not establish:**

- absence of a visually similar result equals legal clearance;
- the database is exhaustive for every jurisdiction/right.

---

### [BI14] WIPO image-based trademark search

**Source:** WIPO press release / Global Brand Database capability  
**URL:** https://www.wipo.int/pressroom/en/articles/2014/article_0007.html

**Supports:**

- official infrastructure explicitly provides image-based search for visually similar marks;
- similarity search can supplement text and classification searches.

**Does not establish:**

- automated similarity ranking is a legal infringement test.

---

### [BI15] EUIPO — figurative threshold of distinctiveness

**Source:** EUIPO Trade Mark Guidelines  
**URL:** https://guidelines.euipo.europa.eu/1935303/2042666/trade-mark-guidelines/4-2-assessment-of-the-figurative-threshold

**Supports:**

- simple geometric shapes, commonplace figurative elements, relation to goods/services, typography, color, placement, and combination can affect whether figurative features add distinctiveness;
- distinctiveness is assessed in relation to the applied-for goods/services and the mark as a whole.

**Does not establish:**

- a general design heuristic that “more complex = more registrable”;
- trademark clearance outside the applicable legal process.

## D. Professional identity-system practice

These sources are used as **practice evidence**, not causal marketing evidence.

### [BI16] Pentagram — Pfizer

**URL:** https://www.pentagram.com/work/pfizer

**Supports:**

- professional identity work can start from purpose/personality and extend through typography, color, photography, iconography, data visualization, motion, web/product applications, templates, and governance;
- a visual identity system can be scalable without homogenizing every sub-brand/application.

**Does not establish:**

- Pfizer’s specific design choices are universal best practices.

---

### [BI17] Pentagram — EDP

**URL:** https://www.pentagram.com/work/edp

**Supports:**

- a core symbol can become part of a broader motion and cross-channel brand language;
- identity may originate from a generative/motion concept rather than a static logo-first process.

**Does not establish:**

- every identity needs motion or a symbolic brand story.

---

### [BI18] Pentagram — Sustainability Solutions Group

**URL:** https://www.pentagram.com/work/sustainability-solutions-group

**Supports:**

- category visual conventions can be deliberately audited and challenged;
- research/interviews can identify a strategic expression that then propagates through wordmark, type, iconography, data visualization, photography, and color.

**Does not establish:**

- “break category conventions” is always the correct identity strategy.

---

### [BI19] Pentagram — Intrinsic / Satalia

**URLs:**  
https://www.pentagram.com/work/intrinsic  
https://www.pentagram.com/work/satalia/story

**Supports:**

- technology/AI identities can use a coherent visual mechanism across wordmark, symbol, icons, motion, type, and product contexts rather than relying on generic AI motifs;
- marks are evaluated at both large expressive and small functional scales.

**Does not establish:**

- any specific aesthetic as the correct visual language for AI products.

## E. Production / implementation guidance

### [BI20] IBM Design Language — IBM logo usage

**URL:** https://www.ibm.com/design/language/ibm-logos/8-bar/

**Supports:**

- identity deployment requires rules for clear space, positive/reversed use, alignment, and integrity across contexts;
- production rules are part of maintaining a mark, not merely cosmetic documentation.

**Does not establish:**

- IBM’s exact ratios as universal logo rules.

---

### [BI21] IBM Design Language — App icons / UI icons

**URLs:**  
https://www.ibm.com/design/language/iconography/app-icons/usage/  
https://www.ibm.com/design/language/iconography/ui-icons/usage/

**Supports:**

- small digital sizes require explicit optimization, live-area/padding decisions, and optical rather than purely mechanical alignment;
- brand marks and functional icons have different jobs and should not be conflated.

**Does not establish:**

- IBM’s 16/20/24/32 px system as a universal mandatory scale for all brands.

---

### [BI22] Apple — App Icon Design

**URL:** https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2017/822/

**Supports:**

- app icons require fast readability, clear metaphor/identity, deliberate lineage, and careful refinement over time;
- preserving recognizable lineage can matter when evolving an icon.

**Does not establish:**

- app-icon principles as a complete corporate-identity method.

## F. Generative-image research

### [BI23] LogoDiffuser (2026)

**Title:** LogoDiffuser: Training-Free Multilingual Logo Generation and Stylization via Letter-Aware Attention Control  
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09759

**Supports:**

- text/character geometry preservation remains a specific technical challenge in generative logo systems;
- structural control requires mechanisms beyond unconstrained text prompting.

**Does not establish:**

- all generative models fail at typography;
- raster generation cannot be useful for concept exploration.

---

### [BI24] LogoSticker (2024)

**Title:** LogoSticker: Inserting Logos into Diffusion Models for Customized Generation  
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13752

**Supports:**

- precise logo identity preservation in generative contexts is a specialized problem;
- logos behave differently from generic visual concepts because small pattern/textual changes can alter identity.

**Does not establish:**

- a specific production workflow for Marketing Practitioner.

## Cross-source synthesis rules

The source map currently supports these evidence boundaries:

```text
MARKETING EXPERIMENT
→ can inform likely perception under studied conditions
→ cannot certify a specific logo’s market outcome

DISTINCTIVE-ASSET MEASUREMENT
→ can establish learned memory linkage when measured
→ cannot be replaced by team intuition for established brands

STUDIO CASE STUDY
→ can show viable professional system practice
→ cannot establish universal causal superiority

OFFICIAL TRADEMARK DATABASE / GUIDELINE
→ can support bounded pre-flight and legal dependency identification
→ cannot provide legal clearance through agent inference

PRODUCTION GUIDELINE
→ can demonstrate implementation concerns
→ should not be copied as universal numeric law

GENERATIVE-IMAGE RESEARCH
→ can justify preserving structure and not treating generation as exact production geometry
→ does not imply image generation should be excluded from exploration
```

## Evidence gaps still open

The next pass should look specifically for stronger evidence on:

1. identity redesign and preservation of existing visual equity outside well-known anecdotal cases;
2. candidate testing methods that separate recognition, attribution, unintended meaning, and preference;
3. wordmark / typography decisions beyond incomplete-letter effects;
4. cross-cultural visual-association portability and when Chapter 07 should become a dependency;
5. color as a brand asset versus color accessibility/production constraints;
6. when a visual identity system should include motion/sonic/character assets versus remain narrow;
7. practical methods for controlled variant testing without creating pseudo-quantitative scoring;
8. rights/provenance concerns specific to AI-assisted asset creation, while keeping legal ownership outside the marketing capability.
