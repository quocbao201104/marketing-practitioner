# Commercial Design Research Evidence Ledger

Status: **research-only evidence ledger**

This ledger supports the commercial-design theory freeze candidate. It does not make these sources runtime rules. Each entry records what the source is used for and what it does **not** justify.

Identifiers use the local `CDxx` namespace so this research pass does not consume or silently redefine runtime bibliography identifiers.

---

## [CD01] Kienzler & Kowalkowski — pricing-strategy research review

Mario Kienzler & Christian Kowalkowski (2017). **Pricing strategy: A review of 22 years of marketing research.** *Journal of Business Research*, 78, 101–110.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2017.05.005

Evidence type: peer-reviewed literature review / content analysis.

The study analyzes 515 articles published between 1995 and 2016.

Use in this track:

- establishes pricing strategy as a broad, mature research area rather than a price-point tactic;
- supports prior-art caution against presenting this track as new pricing theory;
- provides a parent for pricing objectives, strategy, context, and research-method diversity.

Does not establish:

- that the four-dimension practitioner synthesis is complete;
- that any one pricing strategy is generally optimal;
- that findings from the reviewed literature transfer without context.

## [CD02] Nagle, Müller & Gruyaert — strategic pricing practitioner parent

Thomas T. Nagle, Georg Müller & Evert Gruyaert (2023). **The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing: A Guide to Growing More Profitably.** 7th ed. Routledge.

ISBN: 9781032016825 / 9781000915969.

Evidence type: established strategic-pricing text / practitioner conceptual parent, not an empirical study.

Use in this track:

- prior-art parent for offer configurations, bundles, price metrics, price fences, dynamic/yield pricing, pricing policy, negotiation, promotions, competition, price setting, price sensitivity, and financial analysis;
- supports rejection of a novelty claim for much of the proposed commercial-design decomposition.

Does not establish:

- empirical effect sizes;
- universal optimal pricing rules;
- that the repository should copy the book's terminology or architecture.

## [CD03] Moorthy — self-selection and product-line design

K. Sridhar Moorthy (1984). **Market Segmentation, Self-Selection, and Product Line Design.** *Marketing Science*, 3(4), 288–307.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.3.4.288

Evidence type: formal economic/marketing theory.

Use in this track:

- distinguishes direct segment addressability from consumer self-selection;
- shows that relaxing direct-addressability assumptions changes product and price choices;
- provides a conceptual parent for cannibalization and for the `selection / allocation rule` distinction;
- supports treating product/configuration and pricing as potentially coupled rather than a rigid sequence.

Does not establish:

- that every product line should use self-selection;
- a universal number of tiers/options;
- empirical effect sizes for current SaaS, retail, or marketplace settings.

## [CD04] Armstrong — nonlinear pricing review

Mark Armstrong (2016). **Nonlinear Pricing.** *Annual Review of Economics*, 8, 583–614.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-economics-080614-115650

Evidence type: scholarly review/theory synthesis.

Use in this track:

- conceptual parent for quantity discounts, bundle discounts, nonlinear tariffs, price discrimination, product-line pricing, upgrades, add-ons, and screening;
- supports the distinction between scalar price level and broader pricing architecture.

Does not establish:

- that nonlinear pricing is preferable in every setting;
- one universal tariff design;
- a practitioner decision rule without demand/cost/context assumptions.

## [CD05] Schmidt & Bijmolt — hypothetical WTP meta-analysis

Jonas Schmidt & Tammo H. A. Bijmolt (2020). **Accurately measuring willingness to pay for consumer goods: a meta-analysis of the hypothetical bias.** *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science*, 48, 499–518.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-019-00666-6

Evidence type: meta-analysis.

The analysis reports 77 studies from 47 papers and 115 effect sizes after exclusions, with an average hypothetical bias of about 21% in the included literature.

Use in this track:

- supports `stated/hypothetical WTP ≠ real WTP`;
- rejects the folklore assumption that indirect WTP methods are automatically more accurate than direct methods;
- supports method- and design-scoped interpretation of WTP evidence.

Does not establish:

- a fixed 21% correction factor for a new study;
- that direct methods are always preferable;
- that hypothetical WTP research is useless.

## [CD06] Tian & Feinberg — subscription price menus

Longxiu Tian & Fred M. Feinberg (2020). **Optimizing Price Menus for Duration Discounts: A Subscription Selectivity Field Experiment.** *Marketing Science*, 39(6), 1181–1198.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2020.1265

Evidence type: randomized online pricing experiment plus statistical choice model.

Use in this track:

- demonstrates that subscription pricing can require jointly modeling overall opt-in and conditional plan choice;
- supports treating a price menu and duration/commitment structure as a coupled decision rather than a single conversion metric;
- supports the distinction between subscription relationship terms and scalar price.

Does not establish:

- a universal optimal duration discount;
- that the estimated menu from the studied dating site transfers to other subscription products;
- that a modeled optimum is valid outside the tested/design assumptions.

## [CD07] Lewis, Singh & Fay — nonlinear shipping fees

Michael Lewis, Vishal Singh & Scott Fay (2006). **An Empirical Study of the Impact of Nonlinear Shipping and Handling Fees on Purchase Incidence and Expenditure Decisions.** *Marketing Science*, 25(1), 51–64.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.1050.0150

Evidence type: empirical analysis using an online retailer's varied shipping-fee schedules.

Use in this track:

- shows that shipping-fee schedules can affect both purchase incidence and basket size;
- provides a concrete counterexample in which free-shipping promotions generate additional sales but can remain unprofitable after foregone shipping revenue and heterogeneous response;
- supports `sales lift ≠ profit` and the need to evaluate nonlinear commercial terms economically.

Does not establish:

- that free shipping is generally unprofitable;
- that the response magnitudes transfer across categories or markets;
- a universal shipping threshold.

## [CD08] Yoganarasimhan, Barzegary & Pani — free-trial duration

Hema Yoganarasimhan, Ebrahim Barzegary & Abhishek Pani (2023). **Design and Evaluation of Optimal Free Trials.** *Management Science*, 69(6), 3220–3240.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4507

Evidence type: large-scale field experiment plus model-based evaluation.

Use in this track:

- supports treating free trials as access/learning commercial interventions rather than only price discounts;
- demonstrates that trial duration can affect acquisition, retention, and profitability in the studied SaaS setting;
- supports the need for multi-outcome evaluation rather than a universal trial-duration rule.

Does not establish:

- that shorter trials are always superior;
- a universal optimal trial duration;
- that results transfer without considering product, market, and customer context.

## [CD09] Santana, Dallas & Morwitz — drip pricing

Shelle Santana, Steven K. Dallas & Vicki G. Morwitz (2020). **Consumer Reactions to Drip Pricing.** *Marketing Science*, 39(1), 188–210.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2019.1207

Evidence type: six consumer studies.

Use in this track:

- supports the separation of underlying commercial terms from presentation/reveal timing;
- shows that later disclosure of surcharges can alter initial selection and satisfaction in the studied settings;
- supports treating material-term visibility as decision-relevant rather than cosmetic.

Does not establish:

- a universal effect size for all mandatory/optional fees;
- that every partitioned price has the same mechanism;
- an ethical justification for obscuring material terms.

## [CD10] Blake, Moshary, Sweeney & Tadelis — price salience field experiment

Tom Blake, Sarah Moshary, Kane Sweeney & Steve Tadelis (2021). **Price Salience and Product Choice.** *Marketing Science*, 40(4), 619–636.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2020.1261

Evidence type: large-scale randomized field experiment on StubHub.

Use in this track:

- supports `commercial terms ≠ representation of commercial terms`;
- demonstrates that upfront versus later fee disclosure can change quantity/quality of purchases and comparison behavior in the studied marketplace;
- reinforces the need to keep representation ethics separate from underlying economic design.

Does not establish:

- that fee obfuscation has the same effect in every market;
- that higher immediate spending from low salience is a desirable outcome;
- a universal presentation tactic.

## [CD11] Anderson & Simester — long-run promotion-depth effects

Eric T. Anderson & Duncan I. Simester (2004). **Long-Run Effects of Promotion Depth on New Versus Established Customers: Three Field Studies.** *Marketing Science*, 23(1), 4–20.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.1030.0040

Evidence type: three large-scale field experiments.

Use in this track:

- shows that current promotion depth can have different future-purchase effects for first-time versus established customers in the studied setting;
- supports `short-run response ≠ long-run effect`;
- supports preserving customer history/cohort when it can change interpretation.

Does not establish:

- that deeper discounts always help new customers or hurt established customers;
- transfer of the measured effects outside the studied durable-goods/direct-mail setting;
- a universal promotion-depth policy.

## [CD12] Dubé & Misra — personalized pricing and consumer welfare

Jean-Pierre Dubé & Sanjog Misra (2023). **Personalized Pricing and Consumer Welfare.** *Journal of Political Economy*, 131(1), 131–189.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1086/720793

Evidence type: randomized controlled pricing field experiment, personalized-price construction, and field validation.

Use in this track:

- demonstrates that firm profit, total consumer surplus, and distributional outcomes can diverge;
- provides a concrete reason to preserve selection/allocation regime rather than only the resulting price;
- supports keeping customer welfare/fairness outcomes conceptually separate from firm objective metrics.

Does not establish:

- that personalized pricing always harms consumers;
- that aggregate consumer surplus determines perceived fairness;
- a universal normative rule for differential pricing.

## [CD13] Homburg, Jensen & Hahn — pricing authority

Christian Homburg, Ove Jensen & Alexander Hahn (2012). **How to Organize Pricing? Vertical Delegation and Horizontal Dispersion of Pricing Authority.** *Journal of Marketing*, 76(5), 49–69.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1509/jm.11.0251

Evidence type: empirical organizational-design research.

Use in this track:

- supports rejecting the assumption that Marketing universally owns pricing decisions;
- finds a nonlinear inverted-U relationship between vertical pricing delegation and profitability, and a positive relationship between horizontal dispersion of pricing authority across Sales, Marketing, and Finance and profitability in the studied sample;
- supports explicit treatment of organizational authority/governance as context rather than a commercial-design primitive.

Does not establish:

- one universal pricing organization;
- that every firm should distribute authority identically;
- that Marketing, Sales, or Finance should always have final authority.

## [CD15] Rochet & Tirole — two-sided platform pricing

Jean-Charles Rochet & Jean Tirole (2003). **Platform Competition in Two-Sided Markets.** *Journal of the European Economic Association*, 1(4), 990–1029.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1162/154247603322493212

Evidence type: formal economic theory.

Use in this track:

- supports explicit actor-side/payment-flow reasoning when participation on one side affects value on another;
- supports `price to one user side ≠ total monetization architecture`;
- provides a prior-art parent for cross-side price allocation in platform settings.

Does not establish:

- that every marketplace is economically equivalent to the modeled platform;
- a universal take rate or subsidy rule;
- that Commercial Design should expand into full platform/business-model strategy.

## [CD16] Ascarza — retention targeting and treatment response

Eva Ascarza (2018). **Retention Futility: Targeting High-Risk Customers Might Be Ineffective.** *Journal of Marketing Research*, 55(1), 80–98.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.16.0163

Evidence type: two field experiments combined with predictive modeling.

Use in this track:

- supports `predicted churn risk ≠ responsiveness to a retention intervention`;
- reinforces the distinction between customer state/risk prediction and causal treatment effect;
- supports modeling lifecycle as repeated state/action/response transitions rather than a fixed campaign funnel.

Does not establish:

- that high-risk customers should never be targeted;
- a universal retention policy;
- that churn prediction has no value.

# Cross-source synthesis boundaries

The theory draft makes several **practitioner syntheses** that are not direct claims of any single source:

```text
COMMERCIAL DESIGN — 4D
configuration / entitlement
payment / value-capture architecture
relationship / risk terms
selection / allocation rule
```

This four-part decomposition is a project synthesis of multiple established parents. It must not be cited as if [CD02], [CD03], [CD04], or any other single source proposed exactly this model.

Similarly:

```text
COMMERCIAL DESIGN
≠ COMMERCIAL GOVERNANCE
≠ COMMERCIAL INSTANCE
```

is a practitioner boundary synthesized from pricing-organization, sales/negotiation, and state-representation concerns. [CD13] supports the importance of pricing authority; it does not by itself prove this exact three-layer wording.

The following are also project-level inferential guardrails rather than quoted laws:

```text
MARKET-DESIRABLE
≠ ECONOMICALLY ATTRACTIVE
≠ OPERATIONALLY FEASIBLE
≠ PERMISSIBLE
≠ AUTHORIZED
```

and:

```text
PARAMETER UNCERTAINTY
≠ DECISION UNCERTAINTY
```

They should survive independent adversarial review before promotion into governed runtime knowledge.

# Evidence still missing or intentionally deferred

This initial ledger is not a comprehensive pricing bibliography. Before runtime adoption, review should determine whether stronger or additional primary sources are required for:

- B2B negotiated pricing and contract/risk allocation;
- returns/refunds and guarantee policy;
- reference-price dynamics and grandfathering/migration;
- price experimentation under uncertainty and value of information;
- direct versus indirect WTP elicitation for new/innovative products;
- fairness and privacy effects of personalized/dynamic pricing;
- marketing–operations coordination and capacity constraints;
- channel contracts / platform commissions beyond the two-sided-market parent;
- price metric selection in usage-based and outcome-based B2B services.

The freeze candidate should remain explicit about these gaps rather than extrapolate from adjacent evidence.
