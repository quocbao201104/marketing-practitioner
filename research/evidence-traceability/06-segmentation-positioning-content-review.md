## Scope and conclusion

Read [Chapter 02](../../skills/marketing-practitioner/handbook/02-segmentation-icp-and-jtbd.md) and [Chapter 03](../../skills/marketing-practitioner/handbook/03-positioning-and-value.md), checking the shared decision/uncertainty rules in [Chapter 00](../../skills/marketing-practitioner/handbook/00-foundations-and-method.md). This extends the earlier [customer-to-message review](02-customer-to-message-content-review.md).

Two bounded clarifications are justified: make the role of binding constraints explicit in target comparison, and distinguish a customer reason to choose from meaningful differentiation. The existing positioning candidate-comparison process is already useful; no new decision framework is warranted.

These are design findings from source comparison and constructed counterexamples, not observed agent failures. Access to original sources improved but remains partial. This is not a systematic review, validation of the entire strategy chain, or evidence of marketing effectiveness.

## S1 — Separate binding constraints from preferences when comparing targets

Priority: first. Chapter 02 §§3 and 8.

Task: choose between a high-spend segment that requires an unavailable product capability and a lower-spend segment the organization can currently serve.

Current representation: the same checklist contains attractiveness, support burden and capability requirements, followed by a statement that their importance is business-specific. Section 8 warns that an attractive segment may be beyond current capability, but does not explain how that fact constrains the comparison.

Gap: an agent could use a compensating score to let commercial upside outweigh a genuine non-negotiable constraint. It could also do the reverse and invent universal exclusions for merely inconvenient attributes. Existing Chapter 00 guidance mitigates the issue; the correction should operationalize the boundary locally rather than duplicate that entire chapter.

Decision consequence: recommend an infeasible target, or reject a feasible one by treating a preference as a requirement.

Smallest correction candidate: distinguish binding requirements for the current decision/horizon from criteria that can trade off. Compare feasible candidates using the organization's actual priorities and evidence. A target that requires a future capability is a conditional option with that dependency, not an available capability today. For material uncertainty, identify which assumption could reverse the preference; do not fill missing information with arbitrary scores. This last comparison aid is project synthesis, not a validated algorithm.

Source support: [S1 Step 2](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-10-8818-6_4) distinguishes non-negotiable eligibility criteria from attractiveness criteria. [Step 8](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-10-8818-6_10) evaluates both segment appeal and relative organizational competitiveness. Adopt the distinction, not its full process, committee structure or universal list of exclusions.

## S2 — Do not equate every reason to choose with meaningful differentiation

Priority: second. Chapter 03 §1, with §§3 and 10 as consistency checks.

Task: position a credible offering with category-standard functionality and recognizable branding, where no exclusive feature or meaningful comparative advantage has been established.

Current representation: the question attached to differentiation asks why this option should be preferred. That captures a wider choice question than perceived meaningful differences between alternatives. Later menus include a differentiator, although §10 explicitly says they are not mandatory forms.

Gap: reasons for selection can include recognition, access or context without establishing a meaningful product difference. Conversely, meaningful differentiation need not be an exclusive feature; it can involve a supported service or contextual difference. The current short definition makes these distinctions easy to collapse.

Decision consequence: force or invent a unique selling proposition, or relabel recognition as differentiation and lose the two-question distinction the chapter intended to preserve.

Smallest correction candidate: define differentiation in terms of meaningful perceived differences relative to relevant alternatives; keep recognition/identification distinct. Treat reasons for choice as the broader question. Explicitly permit no demonstrated differentiator where that is what the evidence supports. Do not imply either that distinctive cues prove capability/value or that distinctive cues cannot contribute to choice.

Support: the distinctions are informed by [R06's discussion and conclusion](https://assets.marketingscience.info/different.pdf). The proposed wording is a conceptual clarification, not evidence that any given branding strategy will succeed or a rule against genuine B2B differentiation.

## Counterpressure and findings not promoted

- Do not require a full quantitative segmentation study before an early provisional ICP decision.
- Do not eliminate a target just because it is small, difficult to reach, or expensive to serve; whether that violates a binding condition depends on the actual objective and economics.
- Do not add a mandatory decision matrix, fixed weights or numerical confidence.
- Do not require a separate approval when the user already delegated selection.
- Do not infer that a consumer-brand study proves the same effect in enterprise procurement or a new market.
- Do not make every category frame a defect because its full evidence was inaccessible. The existing strategy comparison can preserve it as a candidate.
- Do not add another generic positioning process: §10 already specifies how to compare candidates and when to preserve an unresolved distinction.
