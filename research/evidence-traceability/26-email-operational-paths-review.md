## Cross-chain assessment

The current design preserves communication need, authority and feasibility as separate questions. A positive answer to one does not settle the others. History also survives into message allocation after sending is justified, which prevents the send decision from erasing recipient burden or the sender's actual standing.

The chapter does not require every communication to be a click-oriented sequence. It can support direct copy transformation, complete informational messages, reply-oriented contact, state-dependent follow-up, and a bounded destination handoff. Multi-output completion and provisional decision-making come from the core; a lack of universally optimal timing does not excuse leaving an authorized planning task incomplete.

The inspected paths did not reveal a missing shared primitive, route or controller instruction. Their adequacy remains a design judgment, not evidence that smaller models will reliably apply them. Real client rendering, source-system data quality, current jurisdiction/provider applicability, and delivery execution require task-specific checks when material. This pass does not validate those implementations.
