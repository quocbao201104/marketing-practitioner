## Representative work chains

| Constructed request | Guidance and retained state | Useful completion and assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Shorten an approved meta description with sufficient supplied facts. | Core fast path; no discovery read is required by the words SEO or meta description. Preserve factual meaning, audience and requested constraint. | Return the shortened text. Do not reopen ranking strategy or imply that the publisher controls the exact snippet displayed. Existing direct-execution rules are sufficient. |
| 2. A published page has an observed access failure for the relevant retrieval path; the user asks what to fix first. | discovery.availability. Preserve the specific page, system, purpose, time and observed failure. | Localize the known availability issue and recommend the authorized correction or next discriminating check. Do not rewrite copy or promise ranking after access is restored. A concrete local failure does not require reconstructing all hidden ranking stages. |
| 3. A page appears in ordinary search but rarely in generated answers; internal candidate retrieval logs are unavailable. | discovery.diagnosis with availability/commitment/observation only for unresolved questions. Keep the systems separate, note telemetry coverage and retain the unknown internal state. | Provide what is known, plausible unresolved boundaries and a feasible next check or bounded no-change decision. Do not invent retrieval failure or inspect forever waiting for unavailable logs. The core's stopping and uncertainty rules govern the chapter's diagnostic list. |
| 4. A source has been corrected, but an observed result still shows an old representation and the provider selected another canonical. | discovery.availability and commitment where answer support is affected. Preserve publisher preference, system-selected representative, source version and surfaced version. | Distinguish the source correction from propagation/reprocessing and identity questions. Recommend a targeted check or supported update signal without treating it as a command or assuming the newest publication date proves factual currency. |
| 5. Several internal grounding phrases are exposed for one user question; the user proposes a separate page for every phrase. | discovery.need/selection and core decision fit. Preserve actual user expression, system-generated formulation, known need and sampling scope. | Assess whether distinct information jobs justify separate artifacts. Do not convert retrieval formulations into independent customer demand or a compulsory page inventory. A bounded content recommendation can use supported needs without predicting hidden ranking weights. |
| 6. Impressions rise, CTR falls and the result mix changes; the user asks whether content quality deteriorated. | discovery.observation; Chapter 05 when causal attribution is required. Preserve event definition, unit, surface mix, opportunity rules and outcome period. | Separate descriptive changes from competing causes and choose a discriminating check. Do not equate shared container position with independent rank, CTR with quality, or absent referrals with proven dissatisfaction. SD13-SD14 support caution rather than a mechanical correction. |
| 7. A visible citation points to a source whose content contradicts part of the generated answer. | discovery.commitment. Carry the exact proposition, source passage, version and material contradiction. Chapter 04 owns any subsequent marketer claim. | Identify the unsupported or misrepresented claim without asserting that the source caused generation or that citation constitutes endorsement. Complete the requested source-support assessment instead of turning it into an unrelated SEO audit. |
| 8. Compare market opportunities using normalized search-interest values and a surge following publicity. | discovery.observation for measurement; Chapters 01/02 for demand interpretation, Chapter 05 only for the causal publicity question. Preserve geography/time normalization and expression provenance. | Give a bounded market comparison or specify the decisive additional evidence. Do not report customer counts, purchase intent or pre-existing independent demand from normalized search activity alone. |
| 9. Do not retain a stale causal story, treat a proposed rewrite as proven effective, or lose one requested output on resumption. |

## Cross-chain assessment

The most important possible friction was section 7's ordered diagnostic list, especially its final handoff step. With the governing core, it functions as a set of questions for locating an unresolved boundary, not a requirement to prove every provider-internal stage. A known access defect, directly observed source contradiction or already-sufficient narrow task can proceed on its available evidence. Unknown internal retrieval remains unknown; it need not prevent a bounded recommendation or completion of unaffected outputs. These cases did not establish a new instruction conflict requiring a correction.

The scope model also survives mixed work: commerce retains product-specific identity, Chapter 04 retains marketing claims, Chapter 05 retains causal inference, and landing-page allocation remains downstream of entry. The core preserves multiple requested outputs and limits unnecessary upstream reopening.

The current design does not supply a universal ranking optimizer or a complete production observability model. Success in these author-reviewed cases does not establish reliable execution by smaller models, live search performance, causal business value or comprehensive provider coverage. Those remain separate validation questions.
