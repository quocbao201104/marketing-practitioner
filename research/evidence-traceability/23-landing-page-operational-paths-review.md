## Cross-chain assessment

The core retains current outputs and return points; Chapter 11 supplies page-specific allocation. Neither requires every task to traverse all eleven routes. Message, commercial design and diagnosis remain upstream owners only when an unresolved dependency needs them. A simple rewrite can finish directly; a complex page can combine guidance without turning the internal decision record into the deliverable.

Reading order remains a design constraint rather than an assertion that all visitors read sequentially. A visitor may seek a specific term or act early; the chapter distinguishes action availability from readiness and allows navigation, alternative information order and comparison representations. These cases did not establish a missing primitive or a reason to add a universal page template.

Two practical limits remain. First, a correct architecture specification does not prove that an agent will render an accessible, responsive, fast implementation; that requires implementation-specific verification when such production is requested. Second, the supplied sources do not establish conversion-optimal layout choices for arbitrary products or audiences. These limits do not justify adding unsupported design rules.
