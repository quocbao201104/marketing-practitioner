# Handbook

The handbook is the conceptual reference for Marketing Practitioner. It is intentionally organized as a compact body of knowledge rather than a sequence of prompts or tactic recipes.

## Reading order

1. [`00-foundations-and-method.md`](00-foundations-and-method.md) — marketing as market learning; question types; evidence and decision quality.
2. [`01-customer-research-and-evidence.md`](01-customer-research-and-evidence.md) — research design, qualitative limits, analytical layers, counterevidence, and scope.
3. [`02-segmentation-icp-and-jtbd.md`](02-segmentation-icp-and-jtbd.md) — heterogeneity, targeting, progress, alternatives, and ICP selection.
4. [`03-positioning-and-value.md`](03-positioning-and-value.md) — category, differentiation, distinctiveness, value, proof, and trade-offs.
5. [`04-messaging-proof-and-copy.md`](04-messaging-proof-and-copy.md) — message hierarchy, reader state, proof architecture, copy, and human-writing quality.
6. [`05-diagnosis-causality-and-experimentation.md`](05-diagnosis-causality-and-experimentation.md) — measurement, competing explanations, causal reasoning, and experiments.
7. [`06-organizational-learning.md`](06-organizational-learning.md) — decision history, failed hypotheses, staleness, contradictions, and reuse.
8. [`07-international-marketing-and-ethics.md`](07-international-marketing-and-ethics.md) — localization, cross-market transfer, culture, persuasion, and calibrated friction.

## How to read the handbook

The chapters distinguish three kinds of knowledge:

- **research-grounded concepts**, supported by the bibliography;
- **methodological principles**, derived from established research and analytical practice;
- **practical heuristics**, useful for structuring decisions but not presented as scientific laws.

The handbook deliberately avoids converting every concept into a worked example. Examples are used only when they resolve a genuine ambiguity. The primary structure is conceptual: definition, boundary, method, implication, and limitation.
