# 02 — Segmentation, ICP, and Jobs-to-be-Done

## 1. Segmentation as a decision device

Market segmentation addresses customer heterogeneity. Its practical purpose is not merely descriptive classification but the identification of groups for which different treatment is strategically justified. Academic segmentation literature provides extensive conceptual and methodological foundations for forming and evaluating segments [R03].

A segmentation becomes useful when it changes at least one material decision: product configuration, offer, price, proof, message, channel, sales motion, onboarding, or service model. If two proposed segments receive the same treatment under the decision being made, the distinction may have limited practical value.

## 2. Bases of segmentation

Segments can be defined using demographic, firmographic, behavioral, needs-based, value-based, usage, maturity, role, channel, willingness-to-pay, or contextual variables. No basis is universally superior. The relevant basis is the one that captures meaningful heterogeneity for the decision at hand.

A recurring error is to privilege variables that are easy to observe over variables that explain behavior. In many B2B settings, role accountability, workflow maturity, existing systems, risk exposure, or implementation constraints can be more decision-relevant than broad demographic attributes.

## 3. ICP as a targeting construct

The Ideal Customer Profile is best treated as a practical selection construct rather than a substitute for formal segmentation theory. It asks which customer context deserves priority given both customer value and the firm's ability to serve, reach, and sustain that customer economically.

A useful evaluation frame considers:

- problem intensity or desired progress;
- product fit;
- economic attractiveness;
- reachability;
- strategic fit;
- support and implementation burden;
- sales-cycle complexity;
- compliance or capability requirements.

These factors form a decision checklist, not a validated multiplicative equation. Their relative importance is business-specific.

## 4. Avoid fictional precision in personas

Persona descriptions can support communication inside a team, but detail should be retained only when it has behavioral or strategic relevance. Decorative biography can create false concreteness without improving decisions.

A more useful customer representation prioritizes:

```text
SITUATION
TRIGGER
CURRENT WORKFLOW
CURRENT ALTERNATIVE
DESIRED PROGRESS
CONSTRAINTS
RISK / FEAR
BUYING CRITERIA
DECISION PROCESS
SWITCHING COST
```

The purpose is to reconstruct the context in which choice occurs.

## 5. Jobs-to-be-Done as a progress lens

Jobs-to-be-Done is a practitioner and innovation lens focused on the progress a customer seeks in a particular situation [R04]. It is valuable because it shifts attention from static customer attributes toward the circumstances that make a solution relevant.

A disciplined job formulation identifies:

- the triggering situation;
- the progress or task sought;
- the meaningful outcome;
- constraints and risks surrounding the change.

The concept should not be expanded until it becomes tautological. Statements such as "customers want success" are too broad to distinguish alternatives or guide design.

## 6. Alternatives define the competitive field

The customer's alternative set is usually broader than the vendor's category map. Competition can include:

- direct products;
- adjacent tools;
- spreadsheets or documents;
- internal labor;
- agencies or contractors;
- existing routines;
- delay;
- doing nothing.

This has two implications. First, positioning must explain why changing behavior is worthwhile, not merely why one vendor is better than another. Second, the status quo can possess advantages—familiarity, low switching cost, political safety, sunk knowledge—that product comparisons overlook.

## 7. Segment quality and treatment differentiation

A segment is strategically stronger when it is sufficiently identifiable, reachable, economically meaningful, and associated with distinct needs or response patterns. However, segment definitions should not be treated as timeless. Markets evolve, products expand, channels shift, and formerly distinct contexts can converge.

Segment evaluation should therefore include a treatment test:

> Would recognizing this segment change what we offer, say, prove, price, distribute, or support?

If not, the segmentation may be analytically interesting but operationally weak.

## 8. ICP selection is a choice under constraints

An attractive customer problem does not automatically imply an attractive target. A group may show strong interest but produce poor economics, excessive support cost, unacceptable compliance exposure, low reachability, or a sales process beyond the firm's current capability.

Target selection therefore requires combining customer evidence with organizational constraints. This is an important boundary: marketing strategy is not derived from customer desire alone.

## 9. Scope and revision

ICP and segment definitions should be treated as revisable working models. They should record the market and product conditions under which they were adopted and should be revisited when evidence changes materially.

This is especially important when early-stage firms learn from small samples. A useful early ICP can be a bounded priority decision without pretending to be a complete map of the market.

## 10. Strategic output

A robust segment or ICP description should make the following explicit:

- target role and organizational context;
- situation and trigger;
- current alternative;
- desired progress;
- principal friction and risk;
- buying criteria;
- economic and operational fit;
- reachability;
- reasons for exclusion;
- unresolved assumptions requiring further evidence.

The objective is not demographic richness. It is **decision relevance**.
