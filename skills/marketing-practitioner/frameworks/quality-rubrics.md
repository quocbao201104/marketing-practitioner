# Quality Rubrics

The rubrics provide review criteria rather than numerical scoring systems. A reviewer may use them as checklists or as the basis for a locally calibrated evaluation process.

---

## 1. Evidence Quality Rubric

### Source integrity

- Is the source identifiable and materially relevant?
- Is the observation grounded in the source rather than reconstructed from memory?
- Are derivative artifacts distinguished from independent sources?

### Inferential fit

- Does the method support the type of claim being made?
- Is qualitative recurrence being mistaken for market prevalence?
- Is observational association being written as causation?

### Scope

- Are segment, market, product state, channel, and period explicit where material?
- Are claims transferred beyond that scope without revalidation?

### Counterevidence

- Were contradicting and mixed signals actively retained?
- Can the current conclusion survive the strongest known counterexample?

### Uncertainty

- Are important unknowns represented?
- Is numeric confidence avoided when no calibration basis exists?

---

## 2. Positioning Quality Rubric

### Category clarity

- Can the intended customer understand what mental shelf the offer belongs to?
- Are category expectations and table stakes acknowledged?

### Comparative relevance

- Is the relevant alternative the customer's actual alternative rather than only a vendor-selected competitor?
- Is the differentiator connected to a buying criterion that matters?

### Value coherence

- Is value linked to a specific customer situation and desired progress?
- Is the value proposition prioritized rather than expressed as an undifferentiated benefit list?

### Credibility

- Is there a reason to believe the core promise?
- Are product facts separated from customer desires and interpretations?

### Trade-off

- Does the positioning make a meaningful choice or attempt to occupy every desirable attribute simultaneously?

---

## 3. Message Quality Rubric

### Reader fit

- Is the reader's state at the moment of exposure understood?
- Does the message reflect the reader's task, risk, prior knowledge, and attention budget?

### Hierarchy

- Is there one primary message?
- Are supporting pillars subordinate rather than competing at the same level?

### Proof

- Is proof commensurate with claim strength?
- Is proof placed close enough to the claim for evaluation?

### Objections

- Are the most material reasons not to believe or act addressed?

### Claim control

- Are unsupported, qualified, and forbidden claims identified before copy is finalized?

### Action

- Does the CTA represent the appropriate next step rather than an arbitrary conversion target?

---

## 4. Copy Quality Rubric

Review in this order.

### Strategic correctness

- Does the copy express the intended positioning and message?
- Has prose introduced claims that strategy or evidence did not authorize?

### Relevance

- Is the value legible to the specific reader and moment?

### Clarity

- Are concrete nouns, direct verbs, and stable terminology used where appropriate?
- Is necessary complexity preserved without unnecessary processing burden?

### Credibility

- Are adjectives supported by mechanism or evidence?
- Are numbers, stories, quotations, and comparisons verifiable from supplied material?

### Channel fit

- Does the information density match the format?
- Does the copy respect the task state of interface and lifecycle communication?

### Voice

- Is the prose natural for the intended author or brand?
- Has humanization removed filler without flattening characteristic rhythm or judgment?

### AI-pattern audit

Check for significance inflation, vague attribution, filler analysis, forced triads, synonym cycling, abstract aphorisms, repetitive punchline rhythm, fake candor, excessive formatting, and generic conclusions.

---

## 5. Diagnosis Quality Rubric

### Measurement

- Is the metric definition stable and complete?
- Have instrumentation changes been ruled out or accounted for?

### Baseline

- Is the comparison period or control appropriate?

### Decomposition

- Has the broad outcome been separated into plausible drivers?

### Competing explanations

- Are alternatives ranked rather than ignored?
- Is evidence for and against each explanation explicit?

### Causal language

- Does the design justify causal language?
- Is attribution kept distinct from incrementality?

### Decision value

- Is the proposed next check likely to distinguish explanations at reasonable cost?
- Has reversibility and the do-nothing option been considered?

---

## 6. Experiment Quality Rubric

### Decision linkage

- Will the result change a real decision?

### Hypothesis quality

- Is the proposed mechanism specific enough to be wrong?

### Design

- Are population, control, treatment, and measurement clearly defined?

### Outcome discipline

- Is the primary metric identified in advance for confirmatory work?
- Are guardrails capable of detecting local wins that create business losses?

### Interpretation

- Are null, negative, and inconclusive states distinguished?
- Is the conclusion narrower than or equal to what the design identifies?

### External validity

- Are transfer claims separated from the result in the tested setting?

---

## 7. Learning Quality Rubric

- Is the prior belief recorded?
- Is the decision context preserved?
- Are rejected alternatives visible?
- Is the result separated from its interpretation?
- Are failed and inconclusive hypotheses retained?
- Are scope and freshness explicit?
- Can the record change a future decision, or is it merely archival?

---

## 8. Ethical Persuasion Rubric

- Are material terms visible?
- Is scarcity real?
- Is social proof authentic and appropriately represented?
- Is declining, cancelling, or leaving materially obstructed?
- Are defaults defensible from the user's interest rather than only the firm's conversion objective?
- Is uncertainty disclosed when it would materially affect choice?
- Does optimization preserve qualified outcomes, trust, and downstream welfare rather than only immediate action?
