# Local Adaptation Evidence — Vietnamese Relationship Realization

Scope: evidence for the first reference local-adaptation contribution, `VN-LANG-REL-01`.

This ledger is deliberately narrow. It does **not** establish a general profile of Vietnamese culture, a deterministic address table, a market-wide preference, or a marketing-effect claim. Evidence here is used only to support bounded language-realization constraints.

## [VNLA01] Nguyễn Văn Khang — Vietnamese kinship-term address and public-service communication

**Source:** Nguyễn Văn Khang, “Giao tiếp xưng hô tiếng Việt bằng từ thân tộc và việc sử dụng chúng trong giao tiếp công quyền,” *Ngôn ngữ & Đời sống*, Số 10 (2014). VJOL record published 2015-08-10.

**URL:** https://vjol.info.vn/NNDS/vi/article/view/20274/

**Type:** Vietnamese sociolinguistic scholarship; direct conceptual evidence.

**Supports:**

- Vietnamese address is a sociolinguistic phenomenon involving interaction between social role and communication role.
- Vietnamese address forms come from multiple lexical sources, including personal pronouns, kinship terms, names, titles/roles, and other expressions.
- Kinship terms occupy a major role in Vietnamese address and extend beyond literal family communication into wider social communication.
- Address choice can convey stance, relationship, and communicative intent; it is not merely grammatical person marking.
- Institutional/public-service communication creates a real standardization problem, showing that broad social convention and institutional realization are not automatically identical.

**Does not establish:**

- a deterministic mapping from age, gender, nationality, or status to one correct address form;
- that one address convention applies across every region, organization, relationship, or channel;
- any marketing-effect or conversion claim.

## [VNLA02] Nguyễn Lê Diệu Hiền — Teaching Vietnamese forms of address to non-native learners

**Source:** Nguyễn Lê Diệu Hiền, “Dạy từ xưng hô tiếng Việt cho người nước ngoài – từ học đến sử dụng,” *Tạp chí Khoa học Xã hội Thành phố Hồ Chí Minh*, 1(173), 2013, pp. 51–58.

**VJOL issue:** https://vjol.info.vn/index.php/khxh/issue/view/1647

**PDF:** https://vjol.info.vn/index.php/khxh/article/download/15041/13497/

**Type:** Vietnamese applied-linguistics / language-teaching research.

**Supports:**

- Correct Vietnamese address selection requires contextual and relational knowledge rather than vocabulary lookup alone.
- Social uses of kinship-derived forms differ from literal kinship relations.
- Learners can know individual forms yet combine self-reference and addressee-reference incompatibly, making pair-level realization a material issue.
- Age/status cues may matter in some contexts, but attempting to infer them is itself a documented source of difficulty; they are not a sufficient runtime lookup key.

**Does not establish:**

- population prevalence among Vietnamese consumers;
- that the learner sample represents all Vietnamese usage;
- a universal `older person -> X` or `customer -> Y` rule;
- that the teaching heuristics in the article should override current first-party or organizational language evidence.

## [VNLA03] Nguyễn Thiện Giáp — Distinguishing person pronouns from broader address expressions

**Source:** Nguyễn Thiện Giáp, “Vấn đề đại từ chỉ ngôi trong tiếng Việt,” *Tạp chí Khoa học & Công nghệ - Trường Đại học Công nghiệp Hà Nội*, Tập 60, Số 12 (2024), published online 2025-03-13.

**URL:** https://vjol.info.vn/dhcnhn/vi/article/view/109798/

**Type:** Vietnamese linguistic analysis; taxonomy/conceptual evidence.

**Supports:**

- Vietnamese personal/person pronouns and the broader set of expressions used for address should not be collapsed into one category.
- Kinship nouns used in address should not simply be treated as temporary pronouns.
- A source-language `I/you` distinction therefore does not exhaust the target-language realization problem.

**Does not establish:**

- one preferred expression for a specific marketing audience;
- precedence among organization, community, regional, or individual norms;
- any causal effect from using one form rather than another.

## [VNLA04] Huỳnh Kim Tường Vi & Thạch Thị Hoàng Ngân — Regional address variation in Southern folk language

**Source:** Huỳnh Kim Tường Vi & Thạch Thị Hoàng Ngân, “Từ ngữ xưng hô trong thơ ca dân gian Nam Bộ,” *Tạp chí Ngôn ngữ*, Số 8 (2012).

**URL:** https://vjol.info.vn/ngonngu/vi/article/view/18985/

**Type:** regional linguistic evidence from a bounded folk-literature corpus.

**Supports:**

- Vietnamese address vocabulary can exhibit regionally distinctive forms and expressive values.
- A national-language label is therefore insufficient evidence for one region-neutral lexical realization when regional evidence is material.

**Does not establish:**

- current prevalence of historical/folk forms in ordinary contemporary marketing;
- that any listed Southern form should be used merely because the recipient is in Southern Vietnam;
- a regional stereotype or deterministic dialect rule.

## Evidence synthesis for `VN-LANG-REL-01`

The strongest supported delta beyond generic localization guidance is:

```text
VIETNAMESE PERSON REFERENCE
!= SOURCE PRONOUN SUBSTITUTION

SELF-REFERENCE
+ ADDRESSEE REFERENCE
= COUPLED RELATIONSHIP REALIZATION WHEN MATERIAL

KINSHIP-DERIVED SOCIAL ADDRESS
!= LITERAL KINSHIP CLAIM

LANGUAGE = vi
!= ONE NATIONAL / REGIONAL / ORGANIZATIONAL ADDRESS TABLE
```

The sources justify a bounded realization constraint. They do **not** justify inferring relationship state from nationality, age, gender, market, or broad culture. Current first-party speaker/recipient evidence, an applicable approved form, and authoritative organizational/community norms remain stronger task evidence where they actually govern the artifact.
