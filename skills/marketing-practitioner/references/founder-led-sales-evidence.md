# Founder-led Sales Evidence Ledger

This ledger supports the bounded Founder-led Sales decision runtime in Chapter 16. It is not a sales-methodology endorsement, universal funnel, CRM schema, closing-script library, or evidence that any framework should be applied as a fixed sequence. The chapter synthesizes decision capabilities across sources and preserves stronger evidence boundaries than the source frameworks themselves sometimes require.

Evidence review date: **2026-09-13**

---

## [FS01] Gartner — B2B buying journey and nonlinear buying jobs

**Source:** Gartner, B2B Buying Journey  
**URL:** https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/b2b-buying-journey

**Supports**
- complex B2B buying is not reliably represented as one predictable linear seller journey;
- buyers can revisit buying work such as problem identification, solution exploration, requirements building, supplier selection, validation, and consensus creation;
- digital and human interactions can both participate in helping buyers complete buying work;
- seller stage completion is therefore not sufficient evidence that the buyer has resolved the corresponding decision work.

**Does not support**
- treating the six jobs as mandatory runtime stages;
- assuming every purchase requires all six jobs at equal depth;
- a universal rule for when a founder or human seller must intervene.

## [FS02] Huthwaite International — SPIN methodology

**Source:** Huthwaite International, SPIN Methodology  
**URL:** https://www.huthwaiteinternational.com/spin-methodology

**Supports**
- Situation, Problem, Implication, and Need-Payoff questions are intended as a logical persuasive framework rather than a rigid sequence;
- understanding a problem is different from understanding its implications and the value of change;
- customer-focused questioning can improve diagnosis relative to immediate product pitching.

**Does not support**
- using SPIN as a complete sales operating system;
- manufacturing pain or consequences until a sale appears justified;
- assuming every discovery conversation must ask every SPIN question type;
- treating buyer answers as independently verified facts.

---

## [FS03] MEDDICC — MEDDPICC qualification framework

**Source:** MEDDICC, MEDDIC / MEDDPICC Sales Methodology and Process  
**URL:** https://meddicc.com/meddpicc-sales-methodology-and-process

**Supports**
- complex B2B opportunities can depend on more than product fit, including economic authority, decision criteria, decision process, paper process, pain, champion behavior, competition, and metrics;
- qualification can be treated as continuing opportunity-health work rather than a single opening checkpoint;
- procurement/legal/paper-process work can remain unresolved after a substantive buying decision.

**Does not support**
- treating a completed field or acronym as verified deal truth;
- making MEDDPICC the universal architecture for all sales motions;
- inferring authority, champion status, metrics, or decision process without evidence and provenance;
- replacing account-specific diagnosis with framework completion.

## [FS04] Challenger / JOLT — customer indecision

**Source:** Challenger, “Why are you losing to customer indecision?”  
**URL:** https://challengerinc.com/losing-to-customer-indecision/

**Supports**
- buyer indecision can differ materially from failure to recognize a reason to change;
- increasing fear of the status quo is not necessarily an effective response when the remaining problem is fear of choosing incorrectly;
- recommendations, reduced irrelevant exploration, and legitimate risk reduction can address some forms of indecision;
- additional persuasion intensity is not a universal answer to stalled deals.

**Does not support**
- hiding relevant alternatives to force a decision;
- unsupported guarantees or artificial certainty;
- using JOLT as a mandatory sequence;
- treating all stalls or no-decisions as psychological indecision.

---

## [FS05] Founding Sales — founder-led sales as motion discovery and transfer

**Source:** Pete Kazanjy, Founding Sales  
**URL:** https://www.foundingsales.com/

**Supports**
- founder-led sales can be used to discover, refine, and eventually transfer an initial sales motion;
- early founder selling can generate learning as well as revenue;
- repeatability and eventual handoff matter; founder involvement is not necessarily the permanent operating model.

**Does not support**
- routing every valuable account to the founder;
- treating founder repetition as sufficient proof of transferability;
- subsidizing poor account economics indefinitely under the label of learning;
- a universal ACV threshold for human or founder involvement.

---

## [FS06] GitLab — Proof of Value operating model

**Source:** GitLab Handbook, Proof of Value (POV)  
**URL:** https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/solutions-architects/playbooks/pov/

**Supports**
- a formal POV can be a qualified, outcome-led evaluation tied to a technical or commercial decision;
- product access/trial and governed evaluation are different concepts;
- meaningful evaluations can require named participants, scope, measurable success criteria, responsibilities, evidence, and a decision date;
- evaluation should have a defined closeout and recorded result rather than continue indefinitely.

**Does not support**
- requiring a POV for every sale;
- treating the label `POV`, `POC`, or `trial` as evidence by itself;
- importing GitLab’s organization-specific approval workflow as a universal sales process;
- disguising custom implementation or development as validation.

## [FS07] Gartner — buying-team conflict and consensus

**Source:** Gartner Newsroom, “Gartner Sales Survey Finds 74% of B2B Buyer Teams Demonstrate ‘Unhealthy Conflict’ During The Decision Process”  
**URL:** https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-05-07-gartner-sales-survey-finds-74-percent-of-b2b-buyer-teams-demonstrate-unhealthy-conflict-during-the-decision-process

**Supports**
- stakeholder conflict can be a material buying-system problem even when individual stakeholders appear positive;
- consensus quality can matter in complex B2B decisions;
- stakeholder objectives, assumptions, or external decision influence can conflict.

**Does not support**
- a universal committee size or stakeholder-count target;
- “multi-thread every deal” as a universal rule;
- equating majority sentiment with operational consensus;
- assuming one survey prevalence applies to every segment or buying situation.

---

## [FS08] Salesforce — Mutual Action Plans

**Source:** Salesforce, “The Sales Team’s Guide to Using Mutual Action Plans”  
**URL:** https://www.salesforce.com/blog/sales/mutual-action-plan/

**Supports**
- a mutual action plan can represent shared steps, timing, ownership, and deliverables in a complex purchase;
- buyer-specific customization matters;
- missed dates and changing dependencies can require plan updates.

**Does not support**
- `MAP exists = buyer progress`;
- a MAP as a required artifact for every deal;
- seller-authored tasks as evidence of buyer commitment;
- treating the artifact as ground truth when actual commitments or conditions differ.

---

## [FS09] Harvard Program on Negotiation — BATNA, reservation point, and ZOPA

**Source:** Program on Negotiation at Harvard Law School, Value Claiming in Negotiation  
**URL:** https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/value-claiming-in-negotiation/

**Supports**
- BATNA, reservation point, and ZOPA are useful concepts for evaluating whether an agreement is preferable to available alternatives;
- walk-away logic should be informed by alternatives rather than by the desire to reach agreement for its own sake;
- whether to anchor or make the first offer depends on bargaining information and context rather than one universal rule.

**Does not support**
- reducing multi-issue commercial negotiation to price haggling;
- a universal first-offer rule;
- inventing the buyer’s reservation point or BATNA;
- allowing sunk pursuit effort to move the seller’s reservation boundary without a change in relevant reality.

# Evidence boundary summary

The sources support several durable distinctions used by Chapter 16:

```text
SELLER ACTIVITY
!= BUYER PROGRESS

PRODUCT FIT
!= ORGANIZATIONAL ABILITY TO BUY

PROBLEM
!= CONSEQUENCE / CHANGE CASE

PROOF ACTIVITY
!= VALID EVIDENCE

VALUE RECOGNITION
!= DECISION CONFIDENCE

FRIENDLY CONTACT
!= INTERNAL MOBILIZATION

PROPOSAL / MAP / CRM STAGE
!= COMMITMENT

WINNABLE DEAL
!= DESIRABLE PURSUIT
```

They do **not** establish one universal stage model, qualification score, stakeholder topology, sales methodology, or founder-involvement threshold. Those remain conditional decisions governed by the current buying situation, evidence, resource burden, and reversible next action.
