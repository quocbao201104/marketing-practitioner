# Brand Identity Evidence

This ledger supports the bounded theory in `../handbook/15-brand-identity-and-visual-systems.md`.

It is not a logo-style library. Each source is scoped to the claim it can support, and every entry states what it must **not** be stretched to establish.

Use the evidence-status labels consistently:

```text
EMPIRICAL / ACADEMIC
PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE
OFFICIAL LEGAL / SEARCH INFRASTRUCTURE
```

Project synthesis and contextual hypotheses belong in the handbook reasoning layer; the existence of a source does not promote those methods into empirical law.

---

## [BV01] Henderson & Cote — logo selection and modification

**Evidence status:** EMPIRICAL / ACADEMIC  
**Source:** Pamela W. Henderson & Joseph A. Cote (1998), *Guidelines for Selecting or Modifying Logos*, Journal of Marketing 62(2), 14–30.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/002224299806200202

**Scope / context**

Research on visual logo characteristics and different logo objectives, including recognition and image-related outcomes.

**Supports**

- visual properties can affect recognition and image objectives;
- naturalness, harmony, and elaborateness do not collapse into a single `simple = good` rule;
- different objectives can make different visual characteristics more or less useful;
- logo modification should therefore be evaluated against the current job rather than a universal aesthetic direction.

**Does not support**

- a universal numeric logo score;
- deterministic personality-to-geometry rules;
- copying the study's design dimensions as mandatory runtime steps;
- current digital-production constraints or trademark status.

## [BV02] Luffarelli, Mukesh & Mahmood — logo descriptiveness

**Evidence status:** EMPIRICAL / ACADEMIC  
**Source:** Jonathan Luffarelli, Mudra Mukesh & Ammara Mahmood (2019), *Let the Logo Do the Talking: The Influence of Logo Descriptiveness on Brand Equity*, Journal of Marketing Research 56(5), 862–878.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/0022243719845000

**Scope / context**

Studies the effect of descriptive versus non-descriptive logo content across studied settings, with important moderators.

**Supports**

- descriptive logo content can improve processing fluency, authenticity perceptions, evaluations, intentions, and performance in some studied conditions;
- familiarity and negative category associations can materially moderate the effect;
- `literal/descriptive = universally bad` is not defensible as a runtime law.

**Does not support**

- descriptive marks are always preferable;
- literal category imagery is automatically distinctive or memorable;
- descriptiveness equals legal distinctiveness or legal ownability;
- every identity should communicate product category literally.

## [BV03] Walsh, Winterich & Mittal — redesign and brand commitment

**Evidence status:** EMPIRICAL / ACADEMIC  
**Source:** Michael F. Walsh, Karen Page Winterich & Vikas Mittal (2010), *Do logo redesigns help or hurt your brand? The role of brand commitment*, Journal of Product & Brand Management 19(2), 76–84.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1108/10610421011033421

**Scope / context**

Research on consumer response to logo redesign and the moderating role of existing brand commitment.

**Supports**

- response to redesign is not uniform;
- greater redesign change can generate more negative reactions among more highly committed consumers in the studied setting;
- the existence of prior brand relationship can make redesign magnitude decision-relevant;
- `REDESIGN != RESET` is a defensible precaution against assuming the old asset has no value.

**Does not support**

- never redesign;
- always make the smallest visual change;
- brand commitment is the only relevant moderator;
- any universal safe amount of geometric change.

## [BV04] Brus et al. — marketer judgment versus buyer-memory evidence

**Evidence status:** EMPIRICAL / ACADEMIC  
**Source:** Brus, Hartnett, Faulkner, Driesener and colleagues (2025/2026), *Assessing branding strength: comparing marketer judgement and consumer data for brand identity elements*, Journal of Brand Management 33, 1–17.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1057/s41262-025-00395-y

**Scope / context**

Compares marketer judgments with consumer evidence for brand-identity elements, including Fame and Uniqueness-style asset-strength measures.

**Supports**

- internal marketer judgment can diverge materially from consumer evidence of learned asset strength;
- marketers can overestimate Fame and misjudge consumer associations;
- learned buyer linkage should not be invented from internal preference, visual novelty, or competitor scanning;
- discarding established assets without relevant buyer evidence can destroy recognition that the team did not accurately estimate.

**Does not support**

- expert judgment has no value;
- every new brand must run a large consumer study before choosing an identity;
- a specific survey instrument is mandatory for every decision;
- a newly created candidate has Fame merely because it looks unique.

## [BV05] Henderson, Cote, Leong & Schmitt — cross-national logo evaluation

**Evidence status:** EMPIRICAL / ACADEMIC  
**Source:** Henderson, Cote, Leong & Schmitt, *Cross-National Logo Evaluation Analysis: An Individual-Level Approach*, Marketing Science.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.1080.0462

**Scope / context**

Cross-national analysis of logo evaluation, including both shared evaluative structure and response variation across people/contexts.

**Supports**

- some logo-design dimensions can be evaluated through common constructs across countries;
- responses are not perfectly invariant across national contexts;
- local differences may matter when they actually change the open identity decision;
- portability and local variation are empirical questions rather than automatic assumptions.

**Does not support**

- every country requires a localized logo;
- nationality predicts an individual's visual response deterministically;
- country stereotypes should become geometry rules;
- a country mention alone should reopen a resolved global identity.

## [BV06] WIPO Global Brand Database — bounded trademark discovery

**Evidence status:** OFFICIAL LEGAL / SEARCH INFRASTRUCTURE  
**Source:** World Intellectual Property Organization, Global Brand Database.  
**URL:** https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database

**Scope / context**

Official search infrastructure covering participating trademark collections with text, classification, goods/services, and image-similarity capabilities.

**Supports**

- official trademark searching can include image similarity as a legitimate pre-flight discovery method;
- search scope, goods/services, and available collections matter;
- an identity decision can surface possible conflicts or incomplete search scope before consequential commitment.

**Does not support**

- absence of a similar result equals legal clearance;
- the database is exhaustive for every jurisdiction or prior right;
- image-similarity ranking is an infringement or registrability judgment;
- a marketing practitioner may label a mark `trademark safe`.

## [BV07] EUIPO — figurative distinctiveness threshold

**Evidence status:** OFFICIAL LEGAL / SEARCH INFRASTRUCTURE  
**Source:** European Union Intellectual Property Office, Trade Mark Guidelines, assessment of the figurative threshold of distinctiveness.  
**URL:** https://guidelines.euipo.europa.eu/1935303/2042666/trade-mark-guidelines/4-2-assessment-of-the-figurative-threshold

**Scope / context**

Official guidance on figurative elements and distinctiveness in relation to the mark as a whole and the relevant goods/services.

**Supports**

- simple geometric/commonplace elements, typography, color, placement, combination, and relation to goods/services can matter to legal distinctiveness analysis;
- legal distinctiveness is contextual and cannot be inferred from visual novelty alone;
- consequential legal judgment remains an external authoritative dependency.

**Does not support**

- `more complex = more registrable`;
- `abstract = legally distinctive`;
- a generic design score for registrability;
- legal clearance outside the applicable procedure and jurisdiction.

## [BV08] IBM Design Language — identity deployment and small-context practice

**Evidence status:** PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE  
**Sources:** IBM Design Language — IBM 8-bar logo and icon usage guidance.  
**URLs:**  
https://www.ibm.com/design/language/ibm-logos/8-bar/  
https://www.ibm.com/design/language/iconography/app-icons/usage/  
https://www.ibm.com/design/language/iconography/ui-icons/usage/

**Scope / context**

Authoritative professional guidance for maintaining one established identity system across real digital/production contexts.

**Supports**

- identity stewardship can require verified master relationships, clear space, positive/reversed handling, alignment, and small-context optimization;
- optical decisions can differ from purely mechanical scaling;
- brand marks and functional UI icons have different jobs;
- production constraints can be part of preserving an identity rather than mere presentation polish.

**Does not support**

- IBM's exact ratios or 16/20/24/32 px system as universal standards;
- clear space or small-size variants as mandatory for every brand/task;
- causal claims that these production rules increase sales or brand equity;
- IBM's visual style as a template for other identities.

## Cross-source inference boundaries

Keep these source roles separate:

```text
EMPIRICAL LOGO / BRAND RESEARCH
→ can inform bounded perception, redesign, or learned-asset reasoning
→ cannot dictate one universal aesthetic

BUYER-MEMORY EVIDENCE
→ can establish learned linkage within its measured scope
→ cannot be replaced by internal preference or visual competitor scans

OFFICIAL TRADEMARK SEARCH / GUIDANCE
→ can support bounded pre-flight and dependency identification
→ cannot become agent-issued legal clearance

PROFESSIONAL PRODUCTION GUIDANCE
→ can establish viable stewardship practice
→ cannot become universal numeric law or causal marketing proof
```

The runtime should not infer a stronger claim merely because several source classes mention superficially similar visual concepts.
